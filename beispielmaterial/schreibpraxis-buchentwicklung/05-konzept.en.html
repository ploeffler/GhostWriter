05 · Agreeing the concept

An initial book concept

Before planning continues, the conversation is reflected back. Open decisions remain visible.

GhostWriter

Let me summarise: under the working title “Ein Bild vom Schweigen”, we are developing a German-language crime novel for adults. Mara restores photographs and discovers an old lie by her mother in the context of a recent death. The conflict over a false alibi connects the two time periods. Does that capture the story you want to tell?

Author

Yes. But please do not immediately present Mara as an amateur detective. She starts asking questions because of her mother. That is what draws her into the murder case.

GhostWriter

I’ll record it that way: Mara’s first motivation is her relationship with her mother. Her inquiries grow out of that. Next we could clarify who the alibi was for and what happened back then. Does that work?

Author

Yes, let’s continue there. Chapters and scenes afterwards.

A useful next step

The summary is corrected rather than waved through as a finished result. The next question follows the biggest gap in the story: the earlier event and its connection to the present-day murder.

Readers / lengthAdults; desired length about 250–300 pages, not yet calculated
Setting / timeFictional small town on an Austrian lake; present day, autumn
Main characterMara, around her late thirties, photograph restorer, returning to her mother’s business
Other charactersMother with a false alibi; Jonas, police officer and old school friend; missing woman’s brother; murdered former ferry operator
Core plotA photograph contradicts the mother. Her old lie draws Mara towards the current death.
NarrationClose third-person perspective through Mara, past tense; precise observation and dry humour
LimitsNo constant violence, no serial killer; take the missing woman seriously as a person
Still openWho the alibi was for; actual past events; culprit, murder sequence, clues, chapters and sections

This simulates idea and concept development. No real book is created, no manuscript is written, and no structure or writing approval is granted.
