03 · Developing the mystery

What connects the two cases?

The starting idea now becomes a possible plot. Suggestions remain clearly identifiable as suggestions.

GhostWriter

We have a recent death, an old disappearance and a photograph that contradicts the mother. Would you like to decide the solution now, or explore possibilities first?

Author

Possibilities. The mother should not be the murderer. But not entirely innocent either.

GhostWriter

One possibility: she gave someone an alibi back then. Another: she witnessed an accident and kept quiet. Which direction fits the conflict between her and Mara better?

Author

The false alibi. For someone she wanted to protect. Not out of greed.

GhostWriter

How could the dead property developer be connected with that old alibi?

Author

He operated the ferry back then. Now he has been threatening someone with what he knows. The demolition would also make part of the old story disappear for good.

GhostWriter

Then Mara could initially pursue her mother’s lie while Jonas investigates the current death. At what point should Mara realise that her mother is not at the centre of everything?

Author

When she understands that the alibi was not for the person she suspected. I want to leave open who was behind it and how the murder happened.

A plot direction, not a finished solution

Mother and daughter carry the personal conflict; the false alibi connects past and present. The author chooses and narrows a suggestion. The culprit, sequence of events and concrete chain of evidence remain open.

Further planning needs to clarify the solution and fairly planted clues. A complete chapter structure would be premature at this point.
