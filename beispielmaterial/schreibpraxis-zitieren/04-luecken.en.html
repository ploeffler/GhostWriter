5 · Missing material

A gap in the evidence remains a gap

Not every old source note leads to an original you can read today.

Author

Suppose my folder also listed a study, but its full text was unavailable. Could we still list it as something we had read?

GhostWriter

No. We can retain the old note as an unchecked research lead. For the new text, we use evidence whose relevant content we have actually checked, or obtain the missing text. A source known only through your book does not thereby become a source we have read ourselves.

Author

Then leave the unchecked study out of this paragraph’s references. Mark it as open in the working notes.

GhostWriter

That is the intended simulated working state. If an indirect citation were needed, it would have to be identified as such; we would never imply we had consulted the original ourselves. The checked expert information is sufficient for this paragraph.

Open, not invented

The hypothetical study is not given an invented title, author, year or page number. No actual working note is saved.

Your own book needs checking too

If an earlier edition is unavailable, its exact wording or page reference remains open as well. The simulation does not replace that check.

Q1 · Expert source actually consulted

Swiss Seismological Service (SED) at ETH Zurich: Can earthquakes be predicted? In particular, the introduction and the “Earthquake early warning” section. No publication date displayed; accessed on 10 November 2026.

Institutional expert information, not an original research study. A factual check for this example, not a complete literature review.
