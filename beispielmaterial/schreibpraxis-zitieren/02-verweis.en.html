3 · Which reference?

Your own book or the underlying expert source?

Choose a reference according to what readers should be able to trace.

Author

I want to support the factual explanation in the new book. I also want readers to find the fuller discussion in my earlier book.

GhostWriter

Then two separate references make sense: an expert source alongside the factual explanation, plus a reference to your own book for the fuller discussion. Your earlier book records your presentation; it does not replace a fresh check of the factual evidence.

Author

Does that make the SED page the original research study?

GhostWriter

No. We are reading the institutional expert information itself, rather than relying on your book’s account of it. But it is not an original study. If a claim depends on a particular research finding, we would need to obtain and check that study. For this basic explanation, we will use the clearly identified expert information.

Purpose and reference
“This is how I presented it then”Refer to your own book with a verifiable location.
Explain a factual claimCite the suitable expert source actually read. Here, Q1.
Offer further readingMention your own book as well; keep factual support distinct from the reading recommendation.

The decision in the dialogue

The author chooses an expert reference plus a separate reference to their own work. The type of source is named accurately.

Q1 · Expert source actually consulted

Swiss Seismological Service (SED) at ETH Zurich: Can earthquakes be predicted? In particular, the introduction and the “Earthquake early warning” section. No publication date displayed; accessed on 10 November 2026.

Institutional expert information, not an original research study. A factual check for this example, not a complete literature review.
