2 · The finding

Separate the passage from its research

Answering “What did I write?” requires the wording and where it came from.

GhostWriter

In the example manuscript “Wenn der Boden sich bewegt”, chapter “Warnen, bevor es rüttelt”, section “Sekunden zählen”, the sentence reads: “Eine Frühwarnung beginnt, wenn das Beben bereits begonnen hat.” [An early warning begins once the earthquake has already started.] The associated research folder lists the Swiss Seismological Service FAQ. This manuscript version has no page number.

Author

So I had already made the distinction correctly. Could I simply reuse my sentence?

GhostWriter

First, what do you want to do with it: show your wording from that time, summarise your earlier explanation, or support the factual claim in the new book? Those purposes call for different references. I will also check the old source note against the source itself instead of accepting it unchecked.

Three layers of the finding
Your earlier textA fictional verbatim manuscript excerpt. It shows what the author wrote in this example.
The earlier researchA fictional source note in the research folder. It is a lead to follow, not a present-day source check.
The expert sourceThe real SED FAQ [Q1]. It is actually opened and read for this draft.

A precise location, not an invented page number

The book title, chapter and section are entirely fictional. For a published edition, use the edition details and a verifiable location from the actual copy.

Q1 · Expert source actually consulted

Swiss Seismological Service (SED) at ETH Zurich: Can earthquakes be predicted? In particular, the introduction and the “Earthquake early warning” section. No publication date displayed; accessed on 10 November 2026.

Institutional expert information, not an original research study. A factual check for this example, not a complete literature review.
