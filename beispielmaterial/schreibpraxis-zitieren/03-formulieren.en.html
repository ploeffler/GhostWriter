4 · Quote or paraphrase?

How much of the earlier text should stay?

Verbatim reuse and a new formulation need different labels.

Author

First show me what a quotation from my own work would look like. Then I would prefer a short new formulation for the new book.

GhostWriter

As a fictional quotation from your own work: “Eine Frühwarnung beginnt, wenn das Beben bereits begonnen hat.” — from your example manuscript “Wenn der Boden sich bewegt”, chapter “Warnen, bevor es rüttelt”, section “Sekunden zählen”. In English: “An early warning begins once the earthquake has already started” [translation for this walkthrough]. Quotation marks identify the borrowed wording. We could only add a real edition and page number after checking them.

GhostWriter

As a new paraphrase: “Early warning systems respond to an earthquake that has already started. Under suitable conditions, they can warn more distant locations before stronger shaking arrives. [Q1]” This is newly worded text based on the expert information, not a verbatim quotation from it.

Author

Use the new formulation. Keep the reference to my own work separate as further reading, not as evidence for the technology.

Selected form

A paraphrased factual explanation with Q1, plus an explicitly separate reference to the earlier discussion.

Agree the citation style

This walkthrough demonstrates the editorial distinction. In a real book, footnotes and bibliography follow that book’s guidelines. Translations, omissions or changes within verbatim quotations must be made clear.

Q1 · Expert source actually consulted

Swiss Seismological Service (SED) at ETH Zurich: Can earthquakes be predicted? In particular, the introduction and the “Earthquake early warning” section. No publication date displayed; accessed on 10 November 2026.

Institutional expert information, not an original research study. A factual check for this example, not a complete literature review.
