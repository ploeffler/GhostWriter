# One letter, three writing dialogues

## Introduction

**An illustrative simulation about augmented work.** Created on 10 November 2026.

The situation and all answers from the example person are fictional. All AI contributions were written by the accompanying assistant for this script. No new requests were sent to Gemini or Perplexity. The named variants are **staged possibilities, not real provider responses** or predictions of their behaviour.

The method comes from our earlier work: a short starting statement leads to questions, the person adds and clarifies details, and draft texts follow. A suitable version can then be developed together. The actual Perplexity dialogue is incomplete; these invented questions do not retrospectively fill its gaps.

## 1. A simple starting situation

A fictional person wants to write about an everyday difficulty:

> A finished letter has been lying on my desk for a week. I want to post it, but it is still there.

The aim is a short personal passage. We are seeking neither a diagnosis nor a general explanation of procrastination.

## 2. The shared starting prompt

This prompt could be used unchanged with all three participants. Here, it remains a simulation.

```text
I would like to develop a short personal passage about everyday life with you.

My starting situation:
A finished letter has been lying on my desk for a week.
I want to post it, but it is still there.

Start with a short dialogue. Ask about five concrete questions in total,
one main question per message. Build on my answers. Clarify what actually
happens and how I experience it, without suggesting causes, feelings
or a diagnosis.

If I do not know something, leave it open. Do not invent biographical
details or an action successfully completed.

After the questions, I will say “Write the draft now”. Then write about
120 to 180 words in the first person: personal, vivid, understated and
with dry, self-deprecating humour. No list of advice and no manufactured
success at the end. It should sound like a person telling a story
about their everyday life.

Begin with your first question.
```

## 3. Five virtual question rounds

**How to read this:** Each round presents three possible questions. The shared author answer applies to all three variants. This is a deliberate simplification: all three drafts receive the same information. Real conversations would develop differently depending on the follow-up questions.

### Round 1 — Where exactly does it get stuck?

**Accompanying assistant — variant written here:**

> If the letter is already written, what step is still missing before you can put it in the postbox?

**Gemini — simulated variant:**

> What exactly does “finished” mean for this letter? Where does its journey from the desk to the postbox stop?

**Perplexity — simulated variant:**

> Is getting to the postbox really the only thing left, or does something else need to happen first?

**Example person — fictional shared answer:**

> It is signed, in an envelope and addressed. It needs a stamp. The postbox alone would not be enough.

**What we have learned:** The visible standstill involves a specific unfinished intermediate step. This does not yet explain its cause.

### Round 2 — What opportunities arise in everyday life?

**Accompanying assistant — variant written here:**

> Where could you get the stamp, and how does that fit into your usual routes?

**Gemini — simulated variant:**

> What opportunities were there to get a stamp this week? What actually happened?

**Perplexity — simulated variant:**

> Is there somewhere nearby where you could buy a stamp?

**Example person — fictional shared answer:**

> At the corner shop two streets away. I pass it several times a week. When I am out, I do not think about the letter. When I get home, it is there to remind me.

**What we have learned:** There is somewhere to buy the stamp. Remembering at home and acting while out are separate; why this happens remains open.

### Round 3 — How does it become a judgement about yourself?

**Accompanying assistant — variant written here:**

> What goes through your mind when you come home and see it on the desk again?

**Gemini — simulated variant:**

> How do you experience that moment when you return and realise another opportunity has passed?

**Perplexity — simulated variant:**

> What do you say to yourself about the letter still lying there?

**Example person — fictional shared answer:**

> That it is ridiculous. Such a small thing. Then I tell myself I am just lazy. That is about the only task I reliably complete with this letter: giving myself a hard time.

**What we have learned:** “Lazy” is the fictional person’s reported self-judgement. The text may present it as such; it should not turn it into an established explanation.

### Round 4 — Is there a difference in a similar situation?

**Accompanying assistant — variant written here:**

> What is it like when you have to do a small errand like this for someone else?

**Gemini — simulated variant:**

> Can you think of a comparable situation in which buying or posting something felt easier?

**Perplexity — simulated variant:**

> Do other people’s letters also sit there if you are supposed to take them along?

**Example person — fictional shared answer:**

> If my neighbour gives me something to take, I usually deal with it straight away. I cannot tell you why that is different. I do not want to invent an explanation just to make the story neat.

**What we have learned:** A difference has been reported. Interest, responsibility, pressure or a particular psychological cause have not thereby been confirmed as explanations.

### Round 5 — What has been tried, and what is only a plan?

**Accompanying assistant — variant written here:**

> What have you tried so far to take the letter with you, and is there a next attempt you actually intend to make?

**Gemini — simulated variant:**

> What help have you already tried? And what would you try next, without knowing yet whether it will work?

**Perplexity — simulated variant:**

> Have you already made yourself a reminder or asked someone to remind you before your next trip out?

**Example person — fictional shared answer:**

> I wrote myself a note. It is now lying next to the letter. Tomorrow I am going shopping with my partner. I have asked him to remind me about the letter before we leave. I do not know yet whether that will help.

**What we have learned:** The note is an attempt actually reported within the fiction. The partner’s reminder is a plan. The letter has still not been posted.

## 4. The shared drafting instruction

The example person writes in all three variants:

```text
Write the draft now.

Use only our starting situation and my five answers.
Leave open why errands for other people feel easier.
The reminder from my partner is only planned for tomorrow.
The letter has not been posted. Write about 120 to 180 words.
```

## 5. Three possible responses to the same instruction

The following texts are **variants written entirely here**. Provider names serve only to identify them within the script. They establish neither a typical provider personality nor a ranking.

### A. Accompanying assistant — draft written here

A letter has been lying on my desk for a week. Signed, in an envelope, addressed. Apart from the stamp, everything is ready. Unfortunately, that is where the postal service insists on completeness.

The corner shop is two streets away. I pass it several times a week but do not think about the letter while I am out. At home, it reliably comes back to mind. There I can get straight on with the next task: calling myself lazy.

If my neighbour gives me something to take, I usually deal with it straight away. My own letter is a different matter. Why, I do not know.

I wrote myself a reminder note. Now there are two things on the desk. Tomorrow I am going shopping with my partner. I have asked him to remind me about the letter before we leave. Perhaps at least one of the two pieces of paper will come along.

### B. Gemini — simulated draft

The letter on my desk is finished. I wrote it, signed it and put it in an addressed envelope. For a week, it has needed a stamp. The corner shop where I could buy one is two streets away.

I pass it regularly. Meanwhile, the letter stays at home, along with the thought of it. That thought returns only when I am standing at the desk again. Then I get annoyed because the task looks so small and still remains undone. I call myself lazy, which at least gives me an answer that changes nothing.

I usually do similar things for my neighbour straight away. I can describe the difference; I cannot yet explain it.

My reminder note is now next to the letter. I have asked my partner to remind me before we go shopping tomorrow. That would be another attempt. The letter is still there.

### C. Perplexity — simulated draft

My letter has almost everything a letter needs. Text, signature, envelope, address. It still needs a stamp, and our joint project has been working on that modest gap for a week.

I could buy one at the corner shop. Two streets away, on my route several times a week. Only I do not think about the letter when I am out. Instead, it waits for me at home. With it comes the thought that I am apparently too lazy even for such a small thing.

My neighbour can give me something to take, and I usually deal with it straight away. I do not understand the difference from my own letter. It remains part of the story anyway.

My first idea was a note. I got that done. It is now on the desk too. Tomorrow my partner is supposed to remind me before we go shopping. I cannot yet produce a posted letter. A note, yes.

## 6. What we could work on together

One possible response from the **still fictional** example person:

> I like the line about the postal service. I recognise my voice in “almost everything a letter needs”, too. But “our joint project” sounds too grand for me. And I want to keep it clear that I cannot explain the difference with my neighbour.

The person contributes again: choosing, rejecting an exaggeration and keeping an open question open. The following result is an **additional proposal**, not a decision made on behalf of the real author.

### A possible jointly edited version

A letter has been lying on my desk for a week. Written, signed, in an envelope and addressed. Apart from the stamp, everything is ready. Unfortunately, that is where the postal service insists on completeness.

I pass the corner shop several times a week. While I am out, I do not think about the letter. At home, I immediately remember it and give myself a hard time. I usually do similar things for my neighbour straight away. Why that feels easier, I do not know.

I wrote myself a reminder note. Now there are two things on the desk. Tomorrow my partner is supposed to remind me before we go shopping. I do not know yet whether that will help. The letter, at any rate, is not on its way.

## 7. Brief analysis and recommendation

**Recommendation:** Show the three variants as possible ways of talking and writing, and make the person’s subsequent selection a visible part of the walkthrough.

- **Shared facts:** All variants received the same starting situation and the same five answers. The stamp, corner shop, self-reproach, neighbour and previous attempts remain present.
- **Differences:** The questions approach things differently; the drafts differ in rhythm, punchlines and compression. This says nothing about the actual quality of a provider.
- **Check:** No cause is invented and no posting is claimed. Humorous phrases are wording proposals; the person decides whether they fit their voice.
- **Human contribution:** Initial experience, concrete answers, uncertainty, judgement of proposals and decisions about the version.
- **AI contribution:** Questions, organisation, wording suggestions and editorial connections.
- **Limit:** This script demonstrates a possible process. A real provider comparison would require actual conversations with a documented starting assignment.

## 8. Building blocks for a later presentation

Starting statement and shared prompt; five rounds with three question variants and a shared answer; three labelled simulated drafts; selection and correction by the person; a possible edited version and a reasoned recommendation.
