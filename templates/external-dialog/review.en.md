# Review of an external return

## Identification and scope

Record assignment, task, unit, source version, provider, displayed model, date and available parts. Disclose missing parts and departures. Review in the project context; do not substitute the external provider’s self-assessment.

## Observations

- Material: distinguish known information, new author answers or verified findings, provider interpretations and explicitly confirmed interpretations.
- Dialogue: use of known answers, open questions, helpful follow-ups, leading suggestions and exact preservation of answers.
- Result: fulfilment of facts, voice, logic and output requirements; identify specific useful or problematic passages.
- Limits: missing information, unchecked sources, contradictions and differing information gathered across providers.

## Recommendation

Lead with a concrete recommendation, such as “Use the material and revise the prose”, then explain:

1. Use: specific information, findings, images or passages and their basis.
2. Revise or omit: specific wording, interpretations or unsupported claims. Weak prose does not invalidate confirmed author information.
3. Clarify: only questions needed for the next step; say if none are necessary. Do not invalidate an explicitly confirmed author answer solely because the preceding question was leading.
4. Next step: draft, ask a targeted question, test another provider or check a source, respecting existing authority and approvals.
5. Confidence and scope: tie the recommendation to available evidence. Do not infer an overall provider winner from one run or equate richer dialogue material with stronger prose.

Recommendation, author decision, saving and formal approval remain separate. A recommendation alone changes no manuscript or repository.
