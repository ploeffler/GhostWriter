An idea becomes a book

Creating a book is this simple.

From a question to a first book draft with GhostWriter.

You do not need a finished book plan. In this real example, a question about ancient fables leads to an original animal story for children aged eight and up: “Die Zangen und das Gänseblümchen”.

1 · Ask your first question

question: can you access the content of ancient Greek/Roman fables and parables?

That is how the conversation begins. Next, the author names the theme:

theme: don't judge a book by its cover

That is enough to get started here. GhostWriter suggests stories and asks follow-up questions. The search for an old fable develops into an idea for a new story.

You provide the starting point. GhostWriter helps you explore it.

2 · Say what you like — and what you do not

I'm thinking of a book for eight-year-olds

The author chooses the audience. He does not want a wolf:

yes, good, but no wolf (something animal welfare advocates would be comfortable with too)

GhostWriter suggests a stag beetle. His large jaws frighten the other animals, but a curious fly talks to him. The author decides:

let's do that!

The story gets its working title: “Die Zangen und das Gänseblümchen”.

You can change or reject suggestions. Your choices set the direction.

3 · Turn the idea into your book plan

Asked about the tone, the author replies with one word:

empathy

He also chooses the narrative viewpoint:

as an outside observer

The conversation establishes the characters, plot, language and length:

Decision This example
Who is it for? Children aged eight and up, for reading aloud and reading independently
Main characters? A stag beetle and a fly, without personal names
What is it about? Getting to know others, exclusion and empathy
What connects the plot? An upcoming meadow festival
How is it told? From the outside, with clear language, dialogue and gentle humour
How long? Eight short chapters, planned at 4,000–6,000 words

GhostWriter presents the complete structure. It is saved only after the author's explicit agreement; he then confirms its acceptance into the book.

You decide. GhostWriter brings your decisions together into a structure.

4 · Choose how you want to write

Initially, the author wants to work chapter by chapter. Later, he changes the brief:

create all chapters without further input from me

Using the agreed concept, GhostWriter creates a connected draft of all eight chapters. The saved conversation records a review of language, viewpoint, continuity and animal facts.

You choose how closely to guide each step. In this case, drafting all chapters was explicitly requested.

How does hands-on writing work? Develop one chapter together

Illustrative workflow · sample messages to try

You can write yourself or request a draft of just the current chapter. Read and revise it with the AI before moving on.

The original conversation initially agreed on this approach (translated):

chapter by chapter while creating it now

The saved conversation does not establish that chapter 1 was actually drafted separately. The following is an illustrative workflow. These sample messages are not historical quotations.

  1. Choose a chapter.

    Open chapter 1 and clarify what should happen.

    Sample messageShow me chapter 1 and its goal. We will work only on this chapter for now.

  2. Bring your own text or request a draft.

    Paste your writing or ask for a proposal based on the book plan.

    Sample messageCreate a first draft of chapter 1. Keep the outside-observer viewpoint.

  3. Read and make specific changes.

    Decide what works and what needs changing. The AI revises accordingly.

    Sample messageShow exclusion more clearly through gestures and dialogue. Do not explain what the animals are thinking.

  4. Review and save the revised version.

    Read the entire affected chapter again. Save only explicitly authorised changes. Saving, accepting changes and final text approval remain separate steps.

    Sample messageReview the whole chapter for language and viewpoint. Show me the revised version before saving it.

  5. Then move on.

    Once you are happy with the working version, request the next chapter. Final content approval requires its own explicit confirmation after review.

    Sample messageLet's continue with chapter 2. Work only on that chapter again.

You set the pace: one chapter, your feedback, the next version.

Your decision becomes visible in the text

The author chose an outside-observer viewpoint. On printed page 2, the German original reads:

Der Marienkäfer sah auf. Sein Bein blieb mitten in der Bewegung stehen. Dann krabbelte er ans andere Ende der Rinde.

English translation of this excerpt:

The ladybird looked up. His leg stopped halfway through the movement. Then he crawled to the other end of the bark.

The narrator describes what can be seen, without explaining the ladybird's thoughts. Movement and dialogue show the stag beetle being avoided. The daisy agreed upon in the conversation is already beside the seating place.

A short decision in the conversation has a concrete effect on the book.
