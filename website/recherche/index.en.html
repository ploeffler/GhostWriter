1 · The claim

A sentence sounds convincing

A clear statement can promise more than its source supports.

Author

My nonfiction draft says: “Today, early warning systems can reliably predict earthquakes.” Please check that claim. If it is inaccurate, suggest a change with sources. Do not replace anything in the manuscript yet.

GhostWriter

I will focus first on “predict”: do you mean an announcement before an earthquake begins, or a warning once it has already started? I also need the surrounding paragraph.

Author

The paragraph reads: “Earthquakes often catch people unprepared. Today, early warning systems can reliably predict earthquakes. This means there is enough time to reach safety before every strong earthquake.” I mean a warning before the shaking can be felt.

GhostWriter

Then we need to check two things: what an early warning system detects, and whether there really is enough time before every strong earthquake. I will keep those questions separate so that the revision does more than swap one word.

The task

A distinction between terms and an absolute promise need checking. The full paragraph remains visible as context.

What this teaches

The follow-up changes the research task: both “predict” and “enough time before every strong earthquake” need checking.

Q1 · Expert source actually consulted

Swiss Seismological Service (SED) at ETH Zurich: Can earthquakes be predicted? In particular, the introduction and the “Earthquake early warning” section. No publication date displayed; accessed on 10 November 2026.

Institutional expert information, not an original research study. A factual check for this example, not a complete literature review.
