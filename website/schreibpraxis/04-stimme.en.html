04 · Your narrative voice

Making it sound like you

A text can get the facts right and still feel too distant. This is when the way the story is told needs attention.

Describe the voice

The desired rhythm is refined for the book: clearly structured medium-length and longer narrative sentences, with short sentences used deliberately for dialogue, turns and punchlines.

Try it on a section

A summarising account becomes more personal and immediate. Concrete observations, a natural speaking rhythm and self-deprecating images shape the new style sample.

Trying out the voice in the text

Fictional dialogue example · Illustrates the process; these are not original quotes. The sample prose is fictional too.

Author

This still sounds like a report about me. I’m more direct when I tell a story. And a little self-deprecating humour is fine.

GhostWriter

Let me try a short passage: “My desk was perfectly organised. At least every pile of paper knew exactly where it had been standing for months.”

Author

Yes, more like that. But not a punchline in every paragraph. The next part should stay serious.

GhostWriter

Then we’ll keep the image here. I’ll give the next thought a quieter rhythm and longer sentences.

Refine it together

The author judges the sample and adds their own images and scenes. These develop into the full draft. A local change in style does not automatically become a rule for the whole book.

What changes

Narrative distance, rhythm and scene development change the effect. The voice is tried out in the text and refined through feedback.
