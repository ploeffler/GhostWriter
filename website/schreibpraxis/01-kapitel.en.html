01 · The first chapter

Turning memories into scenes

Experiences and thoughts come first. You do not need to arrive with a finished text.

Tell the story and explore it

The author describes a workplace scene and an everyday experience. Follow-up questions clarify the sequence, remembered words and details that remain uncertain.

Making a memory tangible

Fictional dialogue example · Illustrates the process; these are not original quotes. The sample prose is fictional too.

Author

I had pointed out a problem. Afterwards, everyone was talking about how I had said it.

GhostWriter

Can you remember a specific situation? What did you say, and what was the response?

Author

I don’t remember the exact words. But I can tell you what happened in order.

GhostWriter

Then we’ll record that sequence. We’ll treat the uncertain wording as a paraphrase of your memory.

Draft and make it concrete

Once the author gives permission to write, a first draft of three sections is created. Reading it reveals that a general explanation feels too distant. A specific memory takes its place.

Develop the opening

Further feedback changes how readers are addressed and how the opening leads into the first scene. The chapter takes shape through several revisions.

What changes

Collected memories become a coherent chapter. Specific experiences carry the narrative.
